## 1. Goal

The strategist's job for our two cars: every strategy call the pit wall makes, answered by one model, so no two answers disagree (D8):

- box this lap, next lap or stay out (D21);
- which compound, and how many stops are left (D22);
- undercut, cover or overcut (D23);
- box under the SC or the VSC (D24, D25);
- switch to inters or wets (D26);
- split or stack the two cars (D27);
- the plan with its triggers (D31).

The measure behind it is the product goal: beat the game's AI strategist.

Rules of every SP4 section hold: numbers only, no verdicts and no explaining text in displays; positive = good for our car; each unit updates when its inputs change; missing data is `Known`, never 0 (C3); displays only render (C65). Realism: rivals' hidden values are `unknown('realism')` (C8); every part of the model works on what a real pit wall sees of a rival (research 11 §5: tyre age, stops, compound rule, gaps), with degradation by age where wear is hidden.

**What "works" means (S2).** The league's recordings cannot gate the model yet: the human drivers are too erratic for a forecast score to mean much. The gate is synthetic truth (§7.1); the forecast scoreboard runs on the recordings as tracking, split by AI and human, and the model is validated against them slowly, over time.

## 3. The algorithm

```
 view: projection, live state, track file, calibration (priors), driver profiles
   │
   ├─ estimation: calculators, each judgement made once (C43)
   │    degradation · tyre-life · pit-loss · fuel · gaps                         (exist)
   │    driver-noise    per car: core lap scatter, mistake rate and mistake size
   │    rival-stops     per rival: stop-lap window, next-compound odds
   │    race-interaction dirty air against gap, pass threshold, defence cost
   │    track-wetness   the track's wetness now (the game sends none)
   │    race-hazard     SC/VSC chance per lap, SC/VSC split, durations          (4.3b)
   │
   ├─ forecast-field: the one shared builder (C50): view + those outputs → one starting point
   │
   ├─ simulators: pure functions on plain arrays (C50, C2)
   │    lap-model         one car, one lap
   │    race-forecast     the field forward from the starting point, one scenario
   │    strategy-dp       per car: candidate base plans + the remaining-race table   (4.3b)
   │    race-monte-carlo  policies scored over scenarios                             (4.3b)
   │
   └─ analyses: queries against the same starting point and simulators
        forecast-score                    the plain forecast as of each lap against what happened (4.3a)
        strategy (job, C51)               ranked policies for both our cars                          (4.3b)
        undercut · pit-sweep · sc-call    calls from the same model                                  (4.3b)
```

### 3.1 Estimation

- **`driver-noise`** (S7, S8). Per car, two parts, kept apart:
  - a **core spread**: symmetric lap-to-lap scatter, estimated robustly from `degradation`'s residuals;
  - a **mistake process**: a rate per lap and a heavy-tailed positive size, from the laps beyond the core on the slow side and from `timeLoss` incidents.

  Mistake laps are left out of the degradation fit and kept here. A human car starts from its driver profile (§6.1), else the human default, and moves towards its own session values as laps come in (shrinkage, as `pit-loss` does). AI cars share a field-level default: AI drivers are very consistent, and big mistakes or retirements are rare across the whole AI field (one or two per race), so the rate is per field, not per car (S7).
- **`rival-stops`** (S3). Per rival: a window for its next stop lap and the odds of its next compound, from its tyre life (`tyre-life`), the laps left, the compound rule (`required` span: it must still stop) and its stint so far. Rivals do not react to our plan; they do react to an SC or rain by fixed rules (§3.5).
- **`race-interaction`** (research 11 §5). The dirty-air loss against the gap, the pace advantage a pass needs, the defence cost to both cars, DRS gain. No published value is a real-race measurement and real timing shows no measurable net dirty-air loss (Joshi), so every value is fitted on F1 25 recordings (clean following stints, DRS and battle laps apart), starts at zero dirty air and TUM's pass threshold, and is calibrated per track (C62a) and per driver type.
- **`track-wetness`** (S12). The track's wetness now, from the weather, the rain and the field's lap times per tyre type; the starting point of the forecast's wetness state.
- **`race-hazard`** (S6, 4.3b). Per lap the chance of an SC and of a VSC, their split and their durations: the track file's real-F1 rates (§6.2), calibrated over time like the other track values, scaled by the lobby's safety-car setting (Off draws none; Reduced and Increased scale by config factors).

### 3.2 `forecast-field` and `lap-model`

`forecast-field` is the only code that turns the view and the calculator outputs into a starting point: every car's position on the lap, gaps, tyres (compound, wear or age, sets left for our cars), fuel, pace parameters, noise parameters, predicted stops, the teammate's committed policy, the weather samples, the wetness. Every analysis builds its starting point through it (C50).

`lap-model`: one car, one lap. Additive, after TUM and Fieni: base pace + compound offset + tyre term (wear where wear is visible, age where hidden; convex, extrapolation past the data flagged) + fuel (`γ` a fixed prior: fuel and degradation cannot be separated within a stint) + dirty air (from `race-interaction`) + the stop, out-lap and cold-tyre terms + the neutralised terms (SC, VSC: slower lap, less wear and fuel) + the wetness term per tyre type (slicks lose more and more past a point, inters best in between, wets in the heaviest rain; fitted from the field's laps per tyre type in wet recordings, Pirelli's 110 % rule as the default) + the scenario's scatter and mistake draws.

### 3.3 `race-forecast`

The field forward from the starting point to the flag under one scenario (the random draws, §3.5) and one policy per car; positions, gaps and race times per car out. The scenario "no randomness" is the plain forecast.

**Two step sizes (S11).** A car more than a battle range from every other car is stepped one lap at a time. Cars within battle range are stepped from braking zone to braking zone (track file): dirty air along each segment, a pass attempt at each braking zone, DRS on the straight before it, a defence cost to both cars. Cars move between the two modes as their gaps change. Under SC the field queues behind the ghost car; under VSC nobody passes on track. A retirement takes a car out. If the compute spike shows the mode switch misses its budget, the 4.3a spec falls back to braking-zone steps for every car.

Stops: the condition's pit loss (`pit-loss`), the new tyre, the out-lap and cold-tyre terms, a double-stack wait when both our cars box on the same lap (config default; F1 25's real cost is unknown, D27).

### 3.4 Policies and `strategy-dp`

**A candidate is a policy, not a plan (S13):** a base plan (stop laps, compounds) plus triggers from a closed list, each with its threshold and action:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| SC or VSC deployed within a lap range | box (compound X), or stay out |
| rain: our tyre type falls behind the other in the crossover, or the wetness passes a level | switch to inters or wets |
| the rival ahead or behind pits while within a gap | cover, or extend |
| degradation off the forecast by more than a margin | switch to plan B |
| damage costing more than a time per lap | box |

**`strategy-dp`**, per car, searches stops and compounds only (research 11 §4: the error of faster methods is always in energy, never in the pit choice; ERS and fuel stay per-lap targets). Its state: lap, compound, tyre wear or age, second dry compound used, sets left, SC and VSC laps left. Its cost: the expected remaining race time, averaged over the SC/VSC chances (`race-hazard`), so a plan may stretch a stint for a likely SC, not only react to one (Carrasco Heine & Thraves). Traffic comes from the plain forecast. It returns the best base plan per stop count and compound sequence, always with the "box this lap" and "stay out" variants, and the remaining-race table, from which each trigger's threshold is set (where boxing under an SC beats staying out gives the trigger's lap range).

**Rule-based candidates beside the DP's (S21).** The DP minimises race time and sees traffic only through the plain forecast, so a plan that pays through track position is not among its candidates. The Monte Carlo therefore also scores candidates from a closed list of rules, built from the DP's base plans: mirror the rival ahead, mirror the rival behind, cover (box the lap after a rival within a gap boxes), extend (stay out until the rival ahead has boxed and rejoined). The Monte Carlo scores every candidate the same way, whatever its source; each row shows its source.

### 3.5 `race-monte-carlo`

- **Scenarios.** Each scenario draws: neutralisations (`race-hazard`), weather (a timeline from the forecast's rain chance per sample, wider when the forecast is approximate; S12), every car's scatter and mistakes (`driver-noise`), rivals' stop laps and compounds (`rival-stops`), retirements.
- **Rivals react by rules:** a rival in its window boxes under an SC; a rival switches tyre type by the crossover. Whether the game's AI also reacts to a nearby car's stop (covers an undercut) is measured in the data spike (§8); if it does, the reaction joins this list in 4.3b (O8), since undercut gains are otherwise too optimistic. Our cars follow their policies, triggers firing inside the scenario. A committed policy is followed exactly (S14).
- **Common random numbers.** Every candidate runs through the same scenarios; every draw comes from a stream keyed by (scenario, car, lap, purpose), so a policy with one more stop leaves every other draw unchanged. Policies are compared by their paired differences.
- **Adaptive.** Scenarios go to the policies still close to the best; the run stops when the best is clear by the tie band, or at the job's time cap. The run count is not fixed.
- **Re-scored.** The winner is scored again on fresh scenarios before it is shown (the best of several noisy means flatters it).
- **Output per policy:** expected position, expected points, median race time, the position's quantiles, the chance to gain a position, and per trigger how often it fired and what it was worth.

**Ranking (S5):** expected position; policies within the position tie band (config, never below the Monte Carlo's own error) tie and are ranked by expected points (the league's points table in config) within the points tie band; then by median race time. Every row shows all three.

### 3.6 The `strategy` job and the calls

- **One job per feed plans both our cars in one run (S4, S14).** A car with a committed policy (§6.3) follows it, as certain input: it is the only plan in the race pitwall knows. A car without one is estimated in two rounds inside the same run: round 1 against the other car modelled like a rival, round 2 against the other's round-1 best. No state is kept between runs (C31, C32). Each car's search sees the teammate's stops, the shared-lap double stack and the teammate as traffic. The joint search of plan pairs on team points is out of 4.3 (S4).
- **Cadence:** at each of our cars' line crossings and on events (any stop, SC/VSC start or end, a decision); the newest run wins (C51, the existing job runtime).
- **The calls** (`undercut`, `pit-sweep`, `sc-call`) build their starting point through `forecast-field` and run their scenarios through the same simulators. Each run's spec decides which call is quick and inline and which is a job, by its budget.

### 3.7 `forecast-score` (4.3a)

Two parts, the decision part first (S20):

- **Decisions, on natural experiments.** Every stop of any car in the race, AI cars included, tests the model's decision-relevant differences: the rejoin position and gap predicted at the stop against what happened, the undercut or overcut gain predicted against the gap change once both cars have stopped, the gain of a stop under SC or VSC against staying out (against the cars that stayed out). Each with its prediction error per case, split by AI and human car. These are the model's main real-data check: many cases per race, and they measure what a decision rests on, not the whole race.
- **Forecasts.** Per lap L of a race, the plain forecast as of L against what happened at L + h laps, and the "gaps stay as they are" baseline beside it: per horizon the lap-time and position error, split by AI and human car and by basis (wear, age); from 4.3b, CRPS of the Monte Carlo distribution. Mechanism per A5 (carry-over §9): an analysis on demand (C49) on topic `forecastScore.report`; `pitwall score <recording>` reprocesses, subscribes and prints (`--json`). The 4.3a spec checks that the starting point as of lap L can be built from the projection and the view at L (C45).

## 7. Validation and speed

### 7.1 The gate: synthetic truth (`pnpm check`)

- **A test-race generator** in `test-support/`, written independently of `race-forecast` (so recovering its parameters is not circular): races from known degradation, pit loss, pace, per-driver scatter and mistakes, SC timing, rain.
- **Recovery:** each estimation calculator recovers its generated parameters within the noise put in; `forecast-field` and `race-forecast` reproduce the generated race within that noise.
- **Exactness:** `strategy-dp` equals brute force on small races; `race-monte-carlo` reproduces analytic cases (SC counts and starts against `race-hazard`'s tables; position chances of two cars with known spreads); its error falls as 1/√N.
- **Properties:** no stops and equal pace keep gaps; below the pass threshold nobody passes; a stop never gains position at the rejoin; adding a stop never shortens the race time; the plain forecast is one mode of the Monte Carlo distribution; a committed policy is followed exactly in every scenario; a candidate with one more stop leaves every other car's draws unchanged (stream synchronisation).
- **Contract properties** extended to the new units: determinism (C38), rewind (C30, C33), guarded views (C41), behaviour tables (C44).

### 7.2 Real data

Segment tests (C84), one behaviour each (a pass at a braking zone, an SC stop, an inter switch). The scoreboard (§3.7) is tracking: the user sets targets once the AI numbers settle; no test asserts a recording's score (C84). The synthetic gate shows the code is right; only the scoreboard's decision part shows the model fits F1 25 (S20). Race results cannot show it within a season: finishing positions scatter by about 1.5 places a race, so a gain of a few tenths of a place needs on the order of a hundred races.

**Each part earns its place (S22).** A model part (braking-zone steps, the wetness term, driver profiles' mistake process, dirty air, the rivals' reaction rules, the double stack) stays only if switching it off changes the ranking of policies on realistic cases; the compute spike measures it on generated races, and each run spec lists the result per part. A part that changes no decision is left out until a recording shows it would.

### 7.3 Speed budgets (S18; memory rule "speed budgets in the spec")

The rule here, the numbers in each run's spec:
- one tested time budget per simulator and per job, on the long-race builder (20 cars × 70 laps) at lap 1, the worst case; per `docs/testing.md` §6 a test that fails above its target (best of several warm passes);
- a time per frame for every quick calculator;
- the strategy job's budget is a time cap: the adaptive Monte Carlo stops at the cap, and a test checks both the cap and that the ranking at the cap is stable on the test race;
- each run's spec states what its units may add to `pnpm test:acceptance`, against its time after the acceptance-speed fix; jobs run only where a test subscribes to their topic (C49), and whole-recording acceptance tests subscribe to no strategy topic except one dedicated property test;
- a miss is profiled and fixed, never covered by a raised timeout; Rust or WASM only after a measured miss (D9).

## 8. Proofs of concept (S10)

Two spikes, after this spec's review and the user's speed work, before the 4.3a spec; each starts on the user's go. Throwaway code in its own worktree on a branch that is never merged; not a C82 task; findings to `docs/research/12-…` on main.

1. **Data spike** (first): a read-only analysis of the recordings. AI lap scatter and mistake rates; each human's scatter; whether lap time follows wear better than age; whether F1 25 shows a measurable dirty-air loss, at which gaps; the pace difference real passes needed; the real cost of stops under SC and VSC; how the field's lap times per tyre type behave in the wet recordings; how the AI reacts: whether it boxes the lap after a car near it boxes (covers an undercut), and what it does at an SC by tyre age and laps left. Each finding with the amount of data behind it.
2. **Compute spike**: a rough prototype of `race-forecast` (both step modes), `strategy-dp` with the SC states and `race-monte-carlo` with common random numbers and adaptive stopping, on a generated race. Time per piece; how the ranking of about ten policies settles as scenarios grow (100 to 10,000 against a 50,000 reference), with and without common random numbers; the cost of the mode switch; per model part, whether switching it off changes the ranking (S22); whether the rule-based candidates (§3.4) ever win against the DP's.
