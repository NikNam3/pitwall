2026-11-14 (overnight, supervisor). The TyreSets packet (`external/F1 25 Telemetry Output Structures.txt`, "Tyre Sets") sends, for every car and every one of its 20 sets: wear, `m_lifeSpan` (laps left), `m_usableLife` (max laps recommended) and `m_lapDeltaTime` (ms against the fitted set). Step 1 already writes them to the live state (`packages/engine/src/step/state-update/tyres.ts:20–22`: `lifeSpanLaps`, `usableLifeLaps`, `lapDeltaMs`); only `tyre-life` reads one of them (`lifeSpanLaps`, for display). Research 04 named `lapDeltaTime` as "effectively the TUM compound offset k0"; research 20 §7.2 listed both as untested. This file tests them against what research 21 measured.

**Data and code.** `~/dev/personal/pitwall-research/tyre-spike/tyresets/`: `extract-tyresets.ts` (every car's TyreSets at most every 5 s, with lap, pit status, weather) over the 13 recordings of research 21's races and the Suzuka practices; `usable-vs-wear.py`, `human-vs-usable.py`, `deltas.py`, `nu-check.py`, `wetfit.py`, `ai-switch.py`. Measured rates are research 21's (`strategy/q3_stints.pkl`: worst-corner rate per stint, the per-lap highest corner; AI race stints of 5+ laps unless stated).

## 1. Findings

1. **`lifeSpan` = `usableLife` × (1 − wear / 70)**, to the lap, all race (Silverstone car 19: 56 × (1 − 31/70) = 31.2, shown 31). So `usableLife` is the game's laps from new to 70 %, and **70 / `usableLife` is the game's expected wear rate** for that car, compound, track and race distance.
2. **That rate predicts the AI's measured wear:** AI rate = 0.86 × 70 / `usableLife` (IQR 0.80–0.95, 260 stints, 10 races); over 18 session × compound cells, log rate = 0.82 · log game rate, correlation **0.977**, rms 9 %. Track (C3 1.5–2.6 %/lap in full races), compound and the race-distance setting are all in it: Suzuka C2 `usableLife` 14–16 laps in the 19-lap weekend (race and practice) against 29–31 in the 53-lap one, 2.0 ×, where research 21 §13 measured 2.1 ×. Inters at steady wetness sit at 0.97–1.04, slicks 0.72–0.95; the drying Silverstone inters at 1.18 (wear rises as the track dries, research 21 §3).
   - **As a prior at a stint's start** (leave one race out, `prior-error.py`; error of the rate against the stint's measured rate, p50 / p90): AI (260 stints) flat 2.0 %/lap 33 % / 80 %, a per-compound table over all races 42 % / 79 %, **k × 70 / `usableLife` 10.5 % / 24 %**; our drivers (12 stints) 38 / 66, 40 / 66, **17 / 44 %**. That is as good as 2–3 laps of the stint's own wear (research 21 §2: cumulative rate 8–17 % p50 at n = 2–3), before the first lap.
3. **Humans on the same scale:** DR median 1.19 × the game rate (0.95–1.46 over 7 stints; Silverstone's drying inters 2.29), Asym 1.42 (1.10–1.66 over 8). Research 21's absolute human rates run 1.4–7.3 %/lap; against the game rate they spread ± 15 %.
4. **`usableLife` is per car and fixed:** at Silverstone C3 27–29 laps by car, constant all session. Within a session it predicts a car's own rate only weakly (correlation 0.28 of car / field median).
5. **`lapDeltaTime` between fresh sets is the game's compound offset** (dry, AI, median against a new medium): soft −0.40 to −0.76 s, hard +0.4 to +1.7 s (C1 two steps from the medium: +1.6–1.7), inter +4.2 to +4.9 s, wet +6.7 to +7.5 s. The track files' soft offsets are positive at Texas, Suzuka, Zandvoort and Silverstone (research 21 §8); the game says −0.6 to −0.76 s there.
6. **A fitted set against a new set of its compound gives the game's degradation per % wear:** C4 66–84, C3 50–65, C2 31–41, C1 17–26, inter 10–12 ms/%. × the measured wear rate it gives ν within about 30 % of research 21 §5's fits in 10 of 13 cells (Mexico inters 11 against 11 ms/lap; Texas C4 393 against 155 the worst).
7. **The AI switches tyre type when the game's delta for the other type goes negative.** Singapore: all 19 inter → slick stops came on the first lap the fresh slick read below the fitted inter (−0.3 to −3.9 s); the lap-25 stops back to inters came as the inter delta fell to −3.5 … −14 s. Mexico and Miami: wet → inter at the race's first stops, 1–4 laps after the inter read −1.3 to −1.6 s. Silverstone 2026-11-14: the slick delta never went below +1.6 s, and no AI car switched.
8. **Silverstone, the game's own answer:** a new medium against car 19's fitted inters read +13.3 s (laps 37–42), +11.0 (43), +6.2 (45), **+2.1 to +1.6 s from lap 46 to the flag.** Stay out was right by the game's own model, matching research 21's lap-model estimate (1.2–2.6 s/lap). Pitwall's strategy table said mediums on lap 47.
9. **The delta is not a clean wetness measure.** At Miami it stayed at 12.6 ± 0.3 s while the AI's inter laps fell 4 s; at Silverstone it fell 14.5 → 2.6 s in four laps while the inter laps fell 2.2 s. A fit of the lap model's inter slope against it (w = inter excess + slick delta) gives 0.21–0.84 by race, not one value (`wetfit.py`). It is the game's decision variable (finding 7), not a measured lap time.

## 2. What it means for pitwall

- **Wear prior (research 21 §10, the "wear prior without a measured rate" and driver-factor rows; §13 race distance):** `rate = k × 70 / usableLife` for any car and compound, from the first TyreSets packet, before a lap is run, in practice too, at any race distance. k: AI 0.86, a human 1.2–1.4 until the profile has its own k (k is scale-free: it is a profile entry that needs no race-distance normalisation). This replaces the flat 2.0 %/lap, the per-track calibration wear entries and the race-distance table of research 21 §13 as priors; the measured cumulative rate (research 21 §2) still takes over after 3 laps and 5 %.
- **ν prior:** the game's ms per % × the wear rate, per compound, instead of 150 ± 100 ms/lap for all; inters come out at about 10 ms/lap, as measured.
- **Fresh-set compound offsets:** the game's fresh-set deltas against the medium, per session, instead of the track files' `compoundOffsetsMs` (which are real-F1 race medians, research 21 §8).
- **Crossover and the AI's tyre-type switches:** the game's delta of the other type's fresh set against the fitted one is what the AI acts on. `rival-stops` can predict a field switch from it; `crossover` and the DP's rain trigger can show it beside the 110 % line; a track without a dry reference (a restart, a wet start; research 21 §9) still has it.
- **Realism (C8):** step 1 already hides a rival's sets under realism (`state-update/realism.ts:22`); then our own cars' values stand for the field (the AI's spread is ± 0.3 s on the delta and ± 1 lap on `usableLife`). Restricted human cars online send no tyre sets (research 20 §5).
- **Contract:** these are values already in the live state (C8, C12: not signals); a unit reading them declares `tyres` in its `reads` (C41). No contract change.

10. **The AI's dry stops follow no fixed wear or `lifeSpan`** (`ai-dry-stops.py`): Spa 52 stops at 15 / 28 / 38 % wear (p10 / p50 / p90) with 7–17 laps of `lifeSpan` left; Texas 20 stops at 24–35 % with 6–10 left; Zandvoort's 16 were early SC stops at 11–17 %. Unlike the tyre-type switches, a dry stop is not predictable from these values alone (SC laps not separated here).
